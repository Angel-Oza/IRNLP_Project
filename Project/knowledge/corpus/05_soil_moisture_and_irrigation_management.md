# FAO Irrigation & Drainage Paper 56: Soil Moisture Dynamics and Irrigation Scheduling

**Source Authority:** Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) of the United Nations  
**Publication:** FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper No. 56: Crop Evapotranspiration  
**Topics:** Soil Moisture (% Volumetric), Field Capacity, Permanent Wilting Point, Plant Available Water, Waterlogging Hypoxia  

## 1. Physical Hydrological States of Soil Moisture

Soil water status determines root aeration, nutrient mass flow diffusion, and transpiration cooling:

### Key Soil Moisture Thresholds:
- **Saturation / Waterlogging ($\theta > 40\%$ Volumetric Water Content):** All soil macro-pores and micro-pores are filled with water. Soil oxygen diffusion drops to near-zero, inducing root hypoxia, root rot (*Phytophthora*), and anaerobic denitrification (loss of $NO_3^-$ as $N_2O$ and $N_2$ gas).
- **Field Capacity ($\theta_{\text{FC}} \approx 25\% – 35\%$):** Soil moisture remaining 24–48 hours after saturating rainfall when gravitational drainage ceases (matric potential $\Psi_m \approx -0.1$ to $-0.33\text{ bar}$). Optimal aeration-moisture equilibrium for root uptake.
- **Management Allowed Depletion (MAD / Refill Point $\approx 18\% – 22\%$):** When 40%–50% of plant available water has been extracted. Irrigation must be scheduled at or before this threshold to avoid crop drought stress.
- **Permanent Wilting Point ($\theta_{\text{PWP}} \approx 10\% – 15\%$):** Soil matric suction reaches $-15\text{ bars}$. Roots cannot exert sufficient suction to overcome soil capillary adhesion; leaves permanently wilt and photosynthetic carbon assimilation halts.

## 2. Soil Moisture Management Strategies
- **Drip / Micro-Irrigation:** Delivers high-frequency, low-volume water directly to the crop rhizosphere, maintaining root-zone moisture consistently within optimal field capacity bounds (22%–32%).
- **Laser Land Levelling:** Eliminates micro-depressions and water pooling in fields, preventing localized waterlogging in low spots and moisture stress on mounds.
- **Broad Bed and Furrow (BBF) System:** Raised beds for crop planting flanked by drainage furrows ensure excess monsoon rainwater is drained safely while recharging subsoil moisture.
- **Mulching for Moisture Conservation:** Straw mulching or biodegradable film reduces unproductive soil evaporation by 30%–45%, preserving available soil water during dry spells.
