# FAO Soils Bulletin 76: Soil Organic Matter, Carbon Sequestration, and Biological Health

**Source Authority:** Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) & USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS)  
**Publication:** FAO Soils Bulletin 76 / USDA-NRCS Soil Quality Indicators Series  
**Topics:** Soil Organic Carbon (SOC), Humus, C:N Ratio, Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC), Microbial Biomass  

## 1. Ecological Significance of Soil Organic Carbon (SOC)
Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) is the primary foundation of physical, chemical, and biological soil health. Humified soil organic matter acts as a biological nutrient reservoir, enhances aggregate stability, and provides structural aeration.

### Critical Agronomic Functions of SOC:
1. **Water Holding Capacity (WHC):** Every 1% increase in topsoil organic matter increases soil available water-holding capacity by up to 20,000 gallons per acre (~180 m³/ha), substantially mitigating drought vulnerability.
2. **Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC):** Humus possesses a high CEC (150–300 cmol(+)/kg), buffering soil against nutrient leaching and retaining exchangeable $Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}, K^+$.
3. **Biological Activity:** Supplies energy substrates for heterotrophic soil microflora, mycorrhizal fungi, and earthworms, driving phosphorus solubilization and organic nitrogen mineralization.

### ICAR / FAO Soil Organic Carbon Rating Scale:
- **Critically Low (< 0.50%):** Severe biological degradation, structural compaction, poor water infiltration, high crusting risk. Common in continuous monocultures with straw removal.
- **Medium / Moderate (0.50% – 0.75%):** Typical tropical/subtropical cultivated agricultural soil. Moderate aggregate stability.
- **High / Optimal (> 0.75% – 1.50%+):** Healthy, resilient soil ecosystem with robust microbial nutrient cycling and stable crumb structure.

## 2. Soil Carbon Maintenance & Enrichment Protocols
- **Farmyard Manure (FYM) & Compost:** Incorporating well-decomposed FYM or vermicompost at 5.0 to 10.0 tonnes/ha annually restores active biological carbon pools.
- **Green Manuring:** Cultivating leguminous green manure cover crops (*Sesbania aculeata* / Dhaincha, *Crotalaria juncea* / Sunnhemp) and incorporating them at 45–50 days (before flowering) adds 15–20 tonnes/ha of fresh green biomass and 60–80 kg N/ha.
- **In-Situ Crop Residue Management:** Retaining 30%–50% of crop residues on the surface with happy-seeder or conservation tillage reduces soil erosion and enhances humus formation.
- **Balanced Carbon-to-Nitrogen (C:N) Ratio Management:** High C:N materials (cereal straw C:N > 80:1) cause temporary microbial nitrogen immobilization; applying 15–20 kg starter nitrogen per tonne of straw prevents crop yellowing.
