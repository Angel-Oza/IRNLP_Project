# FAO Soils Bulletin 64: Soil pH Reaction and Nutrient Bioavailability

**Source Authority:** Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) of the United Nations  
**Publication:** FAO Soils Bulletin No. 64 / ICAR National Bureau of Soil Survey & Land Use Planning  
**Topics:** Soil Acidity, Soil Alkalinity, Liming, Micronutrient Fixation, Phosphorus Dynamics  

## 1. Agronomic Significance of Soil pH
Soil reaction (measured as pH in a 1:2.5 soil-water suspension) is the master chemical variable controlling nutrient bioavailability, microbial nitrification, and root cell membrane integrity.

### Optimal pH Range for Agricultural Soils
- **Highly Acidic (pH < 5.0):** Severe risk of aluminum ($Al^{3+}$) and manganese ($Mn^{2+}$) toxicity. Calcium, magnesium, and molybdenum deficiencies are prevalent. Microbial activity and biological nitrogen fixation by *Rhizobium* bacteria are severely inhibited.
- **Moderately Acidic (pH 5.0 – 6.0):** Phosphorus availability is substantially restricted due to the precipitation of insoluble aluminum and iron phosphates ($AlPO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$ and $FePO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$).
- **Optimal Neutral Range (pH 6.0 – 7.5):** Maximum bioavailability of primary macronutrients (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium) and secondary nutrients (Calcium, Magnesium, Sulphur). Soil bacterial diversity and organic matter decomposition peak within this range.
- **Moderately Alkaline / Calcareous (pH 7.5 – 8.5):** Free calcium carbonate ($CaCO_3$) precipitates soluble phosphorus as insoluble dicalcium and tricalcium phosphates ($Ca_3(PO_4)_2$). Micronutrient bioavailability (Iron, Zinc, Manganese, Copper, Boron) declines sharply due to hydroxide precipitation.
- **Strongly Alkaline / Sodic (pH > 8.5):** High exchangeable sodium percentage ($ESP > 15\%$), causing severe soil dispersion, poor aeration, and physical crusting.

## 2. Remediation of Soil Acidity (Liming Protocols)
To correct acidic soils (pH < 6.0), agricultural liming materials should be incorporated into the top 15 cm of the root zone:
- **Agricultural Limestone ($CaCO_3$):** Standard neutralizing agent. Application rate typically ranges between 2.0 to 5.0 tonnes/ha depending on soil buffer capacity and clay content.
- **Dolomitic Limestone ($CaCO_3 \cdot MgCO_3$):** Recommended when soil tests indicate both soil acidity and magnesium deficiency.
- **Application Method:** Incorporate uniformly 4 to 6 weeks before planting to allow complete chemical neutralization.

## 3. Remediation of Alkaline and Calcareous Soils
- **Elemental Sulphur ($S^0$):** Microbially oxidized by *Thiobacillus* into sulphuric acid ($H_2SO_4$), gradually lowering soil pH. Typical dose: 0.5 to 1.5 tonnes/ha for neutral-seeking crops.
- **Agricultural Gypsum ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$):** Applied to sodic and high-pH soils to displace exchangeable $Na^+$ with $Ca^{2+}$, followed by heavy leaching irrigation.
- **Organic Amendments:** Application of farmyard manure (FYM), compost, and green manuring releases organic acids and carbonic acid during decomposition, buffering root-zone rhizosphere pH.
