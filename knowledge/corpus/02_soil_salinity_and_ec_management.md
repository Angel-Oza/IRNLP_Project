# USDA Handbook 60 & FAO Paper 39: Soil Salinity and Electrical Conductivity Management

**Source Authority:** USDA Agricultural Research Service (U.S. Salinity Laboratory, Riverside) & FAO  
**Publication:** USDA Agriculture Handbook No. 60 / FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 39  
**Topics:** Electrical Conductivity (EC), Salinity Classification, Osmotic Stress, Leaching Requirement, Crop Salinity Tolerance  

## 1. Electrical Conductivity (EC) and Saline Soil Classification
Soil electrical conductivity ($EC_e$, measured in deciSiemens per meter, $\text{dS/m}$ in a saturated soil paste extract or 1:2 soil-water suspension) reflects the concentration of soluble ionized salts ($Na^+, Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}, Cl^-, SO_4^{2-}, HCO_3^-$) in the soil solution.

### Agronomic Salinity Thresholds:
- **Non-Saline ($EC < 0.8\text{ dS/m}$):** Normal soil condition. Zero osmotic inhibition for almost all agricultural crops. Microbial nutrient mineralization operates unhindered.
- **Slightly Saline ($0.8 \le EC \le 1.6\text{ dS/m}$):** Critical warning zone. Yield reductions of 10%–25% may occur in salt-sensitive crops (e.g., pulses, onion, citrus, legumes).
- **Moderately Saline ($1.6 < EC \le 3.0\text{ dS/m}$):** Severe osmotic drought. Plants struggle to extract water from moist soil due to high osmotic potential ($\Psi_s \approx -0.36 \times EC\text{ bars}$). Yields of sensitive crops decline by 25%–50%.
- **Severely Saline ($EC > 3.0\text{ dS/m}$):** Only highly salt-tolerant halophytic or semi-tolerant crops (e.g., Barley, Cotton, Sugarbeet) can survive. Severe leaf margin chlorosis, necrosis, and stunting occur.

## 2. Physiological Mechanism of Salinity Damage
1. **Osmotic Stress (Physiological Drought):** Solutes in the soil water lower the soil water potential, requiring roots to expend metabolic ATP to synthesize organic osmolytes (proline, glycine betaine) to maintain turgor pressure.
2. **Specific Ion Toxicity:** Excessive accumulation of sodium ($Na^+$) and chloride ($Cl^-$) in leaf tissues causes burning of leaf margins, chlorophyll degradation, and premature defoliation.
3. **Nutrient Imbalance & Antagonism:** High sodium competes with potassium uptake at root transporters, causing potassium starvation; excessive chloride interferes with nitrate ($NO_3^-$) assimilation.

## 3. Agronomic Remediation Protocols for Saline Soils
- **Leaching Requirement (LR):** Applying excess irrigation water of low salinity to leach soluble salts below the active crop root zone:
  $$LR = \frac{EC_w}{5 \cdot EC_e - EC_w}$$
  Where $EC_w$ is irrigation water salinity and $EC_e$ is tolerable soil salinity.
- **Subsurface Tile Drainage:** Installing perforated underground drainage pipes to remove leachate and prevent saline shallow water tables from rising through capillary action.
- **Organic Mulching:** Applying crop residues, straw, or plastic mulch to suppress surface evaporation and prevent upward capillary salinization.
- **Selection of Tolerant Cultivars:** Transitioning to salt-tolerant crops such as Cotton, Barley, or Mustard during field reclamation phases.
